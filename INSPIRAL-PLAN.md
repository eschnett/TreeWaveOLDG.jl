## Ground rules

These hold for every milestone.

### Branches and pull requests

- Milestone `Mk` lives on branch `inspiral-mk-<topic>`, off `main` after
  the previous milestone has merged.
- Commits, pushes, pull requests and merges only when the owner asks.
  The pull request text is prepared and shown first (`CLAUDE.md`,
  "Repository facts").
- Before a pull request: the suite passes plain, with
  `--check-bounds=yes` and on four threads. That is `INSPIRAL.md`'s
  validation step 7, so it is a condition of every milestone and not a
  milestone of its own.

### Documents

- `INSPIRAL.md` is not edited. Where the implementation departs from it,
  or settles one of its **(open)** items, that goes into `CODE.md` (what
  holds now) and `HISTORY.md` (what changed from `INSPIRAL.md`, when and
  why).
- Each milestone adds or extends `CODE.md` sections in the present
  tense, under a new `## Particles` part with `###` subsections that
  follow `INSPIRAL.md`'s order: the particle, the near field, the split,
  the window, the transfer, quadrature, the right-hand side with
  particles, nested boxes, initial data, diagnostics. It also updates "Status", "File
  layout", "Tests", "Measured results", "Possible extensions" and "Open
  questions", and the tables of contents.
- New sharp edges get a one-line rule in `CLAUDE.md` pointing at their
  `CODE.md` section. Candidates are marked "Sharp edge" below.
- `README.md`'s status changes in M3, when there is something to run, and
  again in M6b.
- This file gets a status line per milestone when it merges.

### Code

- `CLAUDE.md`'s sharp edges all hold. Positions come from
  `node_coordinates`, initial data from `fill_at_nodes!`, and norms from
  `quadrature_norm` or its masked form (M3). Tables are built in
  `BigFloat` and rounded once. `v` is truncated in the state through
  the limiters, `wave_rhs!` is unchanged, and the source is subtracted
  after it.
- The right-hand side is a pure function of `(state, t)`. `t` enters
  only through a prescribed path.
- Kernels go through `map_blocks!` where its owned-point range fits.
  The projection needs a range over quadrature points instead (M2), and
  that is the one place a kernel is launched through KernelAbstractions
  directly. It is written so that it could run on a device, but it is
  not run on one.

  **Inconsistent:** `CODE.md` ("The transfer") has the projection's far
  kernels launched through `TreeAMR.launch_by_owner!`, not through
  KernelAbstractions directly.
- Conventions as in `CLAUDE.md`: 4-space indent, about 80 columns,
  prose-first docstrings, testset names that are claims, each testset
  opening with a comment naming the failure it guards.
- Every dependency change updates both `Project.toml`s' `[compat]` and
  both manifests (root and `bin/`).

### Tests and long runs

- The suite stays fast. The inspiral adds at most about two minutes on
  four threads, with coarse 3D meshes and short times.
- Convergence series and anything longer are `bin/` scripts in the
  `bin/` environment, writing to `bin/output/`. Their numbers go into
  `CODE.md`'s "Measured results". The suite asserts only what a coarse
  run can show, and asserted rates are measurements (`CLAUDE.md`).

  **Inconsistent:** `CLAUDE.md` ("Commands") and `CODE.md` ("File
  layout") run `bin/uniform_motion.jl`, `bin/nested_waves.jl`,
  `bin/prescribed.jl` and `bin/projection.jl` in the package
  environment, not the `bin/` one.
- Runs too large for the laptop get a job script in `bin/symmetry/`,
  following the Symmetry conventions: `amddebugq` for runs under an hour,
  `amdq` beyond, `-t 64,0`, `JULIA_EXCLUSIVE=1`,
  `srun --cpu-bind=none`, output under
  `/mnt/beegfs/eschnetter/claude/treewaveoldg-inspiral/`, and stages
  reported to SimWatch. Timings quoted in `CODE.md` come from Symmetry,
  with the node's CPU model.

## M6: Dynamic particles

### M6a: One particle, solved

**Status:** not started. `INSPIRAL.md`: "Equations of motion", "The
acceleration and its derivatives"; validation step 6, first half.

- The averages `Φ̄ₑ`, `∇̄Φₑ`, `Π̄ₑ`: the remainder's part from M2's
  pairing, the other particle's `Ψ_b` and the particle's own near field
  over `ball_rule`, the own near field's self-force from M1's
  coefficients.
- **The `a` solve**: the equation of motion with `m = M₀ + QΦ̄ₑ`, `F_N`
  added, a 3×3 solve.
- **`ȧ`**: the time derivative of the forces, with `∂ₜΦ_r = Π_r`, and
  the one `ä` in the relativistic `vΠ̄` term taken from the predictor.
- **The `ä` solve**: steps 3–4 of "The right-hand side": `wave_rhs!`
  first, then `∂ₜΠ_r` near the particle with the part of `P S` linear in
  `ä`, projected on the support elements, giving a 3×3 system.
- **The predictor** `ä_pred(X)`: the two-body instantaneous
  acceleration (Newtonian force and each particle's `Φ_C` on the other,
  with physical masses), differentiated twice along its own flow with
  nested `ForwardDiff`.
- An `ExternalForce` motion: a single particle with an analytic force,
  for example a central spring that holds it on a circle.

Tests: the solves reduce to `M a = F` at rest with no field; the
predictor equals finite differences of a numerically integrated two-body
orbit; the spring-held particle at `v = 0.05` reproduces M4's circle
forces.

Measured and recorded: the condition number of the `ä` system against
`hₑ`, and `δm_h/M` (the **(open)** stability question, in part).

**Done when** the external-force particle matches M4's circle at each
resolution, and the `ä` system's conditioning is recorded.

## M7: The binary's warm-up and orbits

**Status:** not started. `INSPIRAL.md`:
"Initial data" steps 3–4, validation step 6's flux balance, `ḋ` and
phase convergence; "Expected accuracy".

It runs on nested boxes (below, "The box"). The other way out, a
multi-patch outer region with a radiative boundary, is M8, proposed:
the seven-patch ball with the nested cubes in its cube and a radiative
outer sphere. A radiative boundary alone would not do on a uniform box:
even a perfect one at side 24 leaves uniform `d/12` above a node's
memory (below).

### What the run will need

- **Warm-up**: a `Prescribed` circle, run in segments. Between segments
  `Ω` is updated from the measured radial force, with the phase kept
  continuous, and each segment is a separate integrator started from the
  previous state. Then **release** to `Dynamic` with the radial velocity
  from energy balance.
- **Checkpoint and restart**: the state vector, `t`, the warm-up segment
  and the parameters, as raw arrays plus a TOML header, which needs no
  new dependency, written every
  few minutes of wall clock. A run can then be split into chunks on
  `amddebugq` (1 hour) or continued on `amdq` (24 hours).
- **Job scripts** in `bin/symmetry/` for the run, parameterized by
  resolution, reporting to SimWatch.
- **The series**: three resolutions, `P = 3`, each scaling M3b's layout
  as a whole. Record `d(t)`, the phase, `ḋ`, the flux balance, and the
  convergence rate of the phase against the second order that "Expected
  accuracy" predicts with orders 1–3.
- **The extraction spheres** on a level whose `hₑ` is about 1 or finer
  (16 elements per wavelength), and inside M3b's clean ball for the
  whole run.

It is done when the flux balances the loss of orbital energy, `ḋ`
matches the leading-order formula to `O(v²)`, and the phase converges,
with all of it recorded in `CODE.md`'s "Measured results".

### The box

The binary radiates at the orbital frequency, so `λ = 2π/Ω ≈ 16 d₀`.
Even warm-up plus a single orbit (`t_end ≈ 23` in units `d₀ = M = 1`)
needs a periodic box with a side of `L ≈ 24` if only the radiation that
re-enters through the faces matters, and `L ≈ 48` if the error from the
non-periodic initial data at the faces matters too. With M3's finding
that the faces' error travels at about twice the speed of light, a
clean time of `t` at the window's edge needs a side of about `4t + 6`.
Uniform boxes at `P = 3`, at 180 bytes per node (M3):

| `hₑ` | nodes, `L = 24` | memory | nodes, `L = 48` | memory |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `d/4` | `5.7·10⁷` | 10 GB | `4.5·10⁸` | 81 GB |
| `d/6` | `1.9·10⁸` | 34 GB | `1.5·10⁹` | 270 GB |
| `d/8` | `4.5·10⁸` | 81 GB | `3.6·10⁹` | 650 GB |
| `d/12` | `1.5·10⁹` | 270 GB | `1.2·10¹⁰` | 2.2 TB |
| `d/16` | `3.6·10⁹` | 650 GB | — | — |

A Symmetry AMD node has 256 GB (cn112 has 384 GB). So `INSPIRAL.md`'s
`d/8, d/12, d/16` do not fit uniformly even for one orbit, and one orbit
would not separate the inspiral from the eccentricity anyway.

On nested boxes (the estimate in `CODE.md`, "Nested boxes") the warm-up
and five orbits, `t ≈ 90`, need a clean time of about 90 at the window's edge: seven levels, side
512, clean to about 169 at the speed M3b measured. That is `1.0·10⁸`
nodes and 19 GB at `d/8`, 64 GB at `d/12` and 151 GB at `d/16`, all on
one node. The binary carries no net charge, so M3's drift is absent.
Its field at the faces is a dipole's, so the faces' error should be
smaller than for M3's single charge.

The time is then the source's. At `d/8` a step takes about a second,
mostly the projection (0.19 s per right-hand side for the binary's
source, M2 and M3, against about 0.06 s for the wave equation on
`10⁸` nodes). The run takes about 22 000 steps, so about 6 hours. At
`d/12` and `d/16`, with the source's cost growing with the window's
elements, the estimates are about 1.5 and 4 days, run in 24-hour chunks
from checkpoints. The projection's speedups (`CODE.md`, "Possible
extensions" and "Open questions") matter more here than local time
stepping would.

## M8: The particles in the seven-patch ball

**Status:** proposed (2026-11-09); not started. Not a step of
`INSPIRAL.md`'s "Validation": it replaces the periodic box's outer
faces by the seven-patch ball's radiative sphere (`CODE.md`, "The
seven-patch ball" and "The outer boundary").

The nested boxes keep the periodic box's outer faces away from the
particles for a clean time that doubles with each level (`CODE.md`,
"Nested boxes"). A radiative sphere would remove three things the
periodic box brings, not only delay them:

- the kinks of the non-periodic initial data at the faces, and the
  error they send in at about 1.5 times the speed of light;
- the drift of `Π_r`'s mean at `Q/(γV)` that a net charge drives, since
  Sommerfeld's condition lets a monopole out exactly;
- the clean time as a limit on how long a run can go.

It does not remove the coarse-fine faces' reflections inside the cube,
and its condition is first order: radiation of angular order `ℓ`
reflects at `O(ℓ(ℓ+1)/(kR₁)²)`, and the near zone's static multipoles
`ℓ ≥ 1` do not satisfy it either. Whether that is better than more
nested levels is M8a's question, and a gate.

**The layout.** The particles and the nested cubes go into patch 1, the
cube, whose map `x = Lc(2ξ/n − 1)` is affine, so that `√g` and the frame
`S` are constants there. The six shells stay uniform out to `R₁`. The
window is zero beyond `R₂`, so the source never reaches the shells;
they only carry the remainder out. TreeAMR keeps a glued face
conforming, a leaf and the one across it on one level, so the
refinement stays off the cube's faces, as nested cubes do anyway.

**The shells' cost.** The sphere absorbs only what reaches it resolved,
and the scheme's central fluxes do not damp an under-resolved wave on
its way out, so the shells have to resolve the radiation out to `R₁`.
A shell's tangential spacing is largest at its face's centre,
`2R₁/n_e` with `n_e` the elements across the cube's face, and the cube's
root level has the same `n_e` across. With M3b's 8 elements per
wavelength at `λ = 16`, `hₑ ≤ 2`, so `n_e ≥ R₁`. An example at `P = 3`:

- `R₁ = 50`, `Lc = 16`, `n_e = 64`: the cube's root level has `hₑ = 1/2`
  and is the half-width-16 level of M3b's layout for M4 to M7, so the
  cube holds that layout's three finest levels unchanged. The shells,
  20 elements radially, replace its two outer levels: `3.1·10⁷` nodes
  against their `2.9·10⁷`.
- Each shell node carries more: the coefficient set's `1 + D² = 10`
  numbers (80 bytes) and `ElementTruncation`'s 793 numbers per element
  (about 100 bytes per node).
- At fixed `hₑ` the count grows like `R₁³`: `R₁ = 100` is about eight
  times as much.

### M8b: Refinement inside the cube

**Status:** measured (2026-11-11), before M8a's gate, at the owner's
request; its pull request is open. The code and tests are in, and
`bin/symmetry/nested_ball.sh`'s runs at M3b's scale are recorded
(`CODE.md`, "The seven-patch ball" under "Measured results"): inside the
cube the ball gives the nested boxes' coarse-fine reflections, the
energy holds as it does on the boxes, and the step time and memory are
recorded. The cube–shell faces send back about forty times what the
boxes' face at 16 does at `λ = 16`, and at `λ = 4` the shells need
radial elements of four per wavelength not to trap the wave. The field
side, with no particles.

- **The refusal.** `check_metric_forest` refuses every refined forest on
  a geometry, because the coarse ghost is the adjoint in the flat
  weights, not the `√g`-weighted ones. Where `√g` is constant the two
  differ by a constant factor, so a refined forest is accepted when
  every coarse-fine face lies in a patch whose map is affine (a trait of
  the geometry, `affine_patch(geo, p)`), and refused otherwise with that
  reason.
- **The Cartesian gradient across a coarse-fine face.** `cartgrad` is
  one set with ghosts on every face, which no refined forest has had.
  In the cube `S` is diagonal and constant, so the Laplacian reads
  `F^d = S[d][d] V_d` as the flat path reads its `d`-th set; whether
  TreeAMR's coupling treats each component as it treats that set is
  checked by the energy test.
- **The truncation by block.** On an affine block the `√g`-orthogonal
  projector is the element's own, so those blocks take the element's
  tables and `ElementTruncation` holds only the shells' elements. On
  the nested cubes that saves about 100 bytes per node.
- **The forest.** `sevenpatch_forest(…; refined)`, with `nested_cubes`'
  regions in patch 1's logical coordinates.
- **The time step** stays `oldg_timestep`'s, the finest logical `hₑ`
  over the largest logical wave speed anywhere, which is conservative.

Tests: the energy, with the Neumann sphere (`κ = 0` without `u/R`), an
invariant to rounding on a refined ball; the plane wave converging on a
refined ball with Dirichlet data; refinement in a shell refused, naming
the patch.

Measured and recorded: the right-hand side's time per node on the
cube's blocks against the flat path's on M3b's layout. A flat fast path
for affine blocks is written only if the difference matters against the
source's cost (below, "Left to settle later").

**Done when** M8a's pulses through the refined ball give the nested
boxes' coarse-fine reflections, the energy holds as it does on nested
boxes, and the memory and step time are recorded.

### M8c: Particles in the cube

**Status:** proposed; not started. Needs M8b and a TreeAMR release.

- **Point location, upstream.** TreeAMR refuses `locate_point` on a
  forest of patches, and its refusal names what would lift it: a
  location given the patch of each point. That is TreeAMR's,
  `locate_point(forest, p, ξ)` ("Scope and non-goals"), followed by a
  `[compat]` bump in both `Project.toml`s and both manifests.
- **`InspiralProblem`** accepts a `FullGeometry` with a particle patch
  whose map is affine, in place of its refusal of every geometry and
  forest of patches, and refuses a window's ball or a support outside
  that patch's finest level, as it refuses one outside the finest level
  now.
- **The transfer in physical coordinates** through the patch's affine
  map, origin `o` and scale `a`: block origins and spacings mapped into
  space, so that `hₑ`, `rnear`, `rfar` and the graded bands are physical;
  `P S` unchanged, since the Jacobian cancels in `(1/wₙ)∫Sφₙ`; the
  pairing's integrals times `a³` and the gradients times `1/a`. This
  covers `project_source!`, `pairing`, `support_elements`,
  `SharedNodes`, the finest-level checks and `reconstruct`.
- **Initial data and norms.** `inspiral_initial_state` fills `(1 − W)Ψ̂`
  through `WithPatch` and `physical_coordinates` on all seven patches;
  `ball_norm` takes physical positions and `√g`-weighted volumes.
- **The averaging** stays on patch 1's finest level, where it is the
  nested boxes' unchanged.

Tests: on the cube, the projection, the pairing and the initial
remainder equal a nested-box forest's over the same finest cube element
for element, to an ulp, as M3b's test against a uniform box; a support
outside the particle patch is refused.

Runs, beside M3b's and M4's on nested boxes:

- a particle at rest and in uniform motion past the nested boxes' clean
  time, with the error in the `R₂` ball and the force recorded to then;
- a single charge's `Π_r` mean, which drifts in the periodic box and
  should not here;
- M4's circle at `v = 0.2`, `d/8`, order 3, its forces against M5's.

**Done when** inside the nested boxes' clean time the ball gives their

are recorded against `R₁`. M7 then chooses its outer region (below,
